# Research: Funding & IP Landscape for PFAS Detection Technology

## Funding Sources (Priority Order)

### 1. SERDP/ESTCP (DoD) — HIGHEST PRIORITY
- **FY2026 Solicitation**: "Real-Time Sensors for Detection and Quantitation of PFAS in Soil and Groundwater at AFFF-Impacted Sites"
- **Total budget**: Up to $10 million across multiple awards
- **Timeline**: Pre-proposals due Jan 7, 2025; full proposals Feb 2025
- **Relevance**: Directly matches TRACE-1 product concept
- **Note**: ESTCP is already field-validating PNNL/NJIT portable electrochemical PFAS sensor — validates the market
- **URL**: serdp-estcp.mil

### 2. EPA SBIR — HIGH PRIORITY (when available)
- Phase I: $100,000 for 6-month proof of concept
- Phase II: Up to $400,000 for development and commercialization
- **Current status**: SBIR/STTR programs lapsed Sept 30, 2025 — first time in 40+ year history
- Congressional reauthorization needed before new solicitations
- When reauthorized, EPA SBIR typically solicits April-June
- EPA has previously funded PFAS detection and treatment technologies

### 3. NSF SBIR/STTR — HIGH PRIORITY (when available)
- Phase I: $275,000 for 6-12 months
- Phase II: Up to $1,000,000
- **Current status**: Also lapsed with Congressional authorization
- Relevant topics: Environmental Technologies, Sensors, Advanced Manufacturing
- NSF previously funded Cyclopure (NSF-1831206) for PFAS testing kits

### 4. EPA Emerging Contaminants Grant
- $1 billion/year appropriation (FY2022-2026) for small/disadvantaged communities
- Not directly for technology development, but partner utilities can access funds
- Utilities can use this to purchase TRACE-1 devices

### 5. DOE SBIR/STTR
- Phase I: $200,000 for 6-9 months
- Phase II: Up to $1,100,000
- Topics include: water treatment, environmental monitoring, advanced manufacturing
- DOE FY2026 solicitation slides available

### 6. State Innovation Funds
- California: PFAS Detection Innovation Fund (through State Water Board)
- New Jersey: PFAS Innovation Technology Fund
- Michigan: PFAS Response funding
- Many states have dedicated PFAS response budgets

### 7. Private Funding
- Accelerators: Y Combinator, Techstars (sustainability track), IndieBio
- Angel investors focused on water/cleantech
- Water-focused VCs: Burnt Island Ventures, Emerald Technology Ventures, 2150
- Strategic investors: Xylem, Veolia, Suez (water utilities)

## IP Landscape

### Key Patent Areas
1. **MIP-based PFAS sensors** — Limited patents, mostly academic publications
   - Opportunity: File provisional patents on specific MIP formulations + chip integration
2. **Microfluidic electrochemical sensors** — Broad field with many patents
   - Need freedom-to-operate analysis focused on PFAS-specific implementations
3. **PFAS detection methods** — EPA methods are not patented (government work)
   - Competitive advantage is in the chip design and integration, not the analytical method

### Patent Strategy Recommendations
1. File provisional patent on: MIP-functionalized microfluidic chip architecture for PFAS detection
2. Include claims on: multi-zone chip design, pre-concentration geometry, differential measurement method
3. Consider trade secret protection for: MIP formulation recipes, electropolymerization parameters
4. Budget: $5,000-8,000 for provisional patent filing
5. Timeline: File before any public disclosure (presentations, publications)
